# Ready-to-paste cfo.ai planning prompt

Build a business plan for POA, an evidence-driven oracle-incident investigation and compensation-review product for lending-protocol risk and treasury teams.

The current product is a synthetic-protocol prototype. It demonstrates historical EVM evidence gathering, bounded query recovery, liquidation eligibility checks, exact compensation calculations, independent claim auditing and recipient-bound Merkle proofs. It does not support production protocol adapters yet. No customers, revenue, production cost measurements or willingness-to-pay interviews are verified. Label every commercial number below as an unvalidated planning assumption, not traction or a forecast we have achieved.

Currency USD. Assume $100,000 starting cash and a $30,000 upfront security/adapter-validation reserve, leaving $70,000 operating cash. Model three steady-state monthly scenarios:

- Conservative: 3 customers; $500 subscription per customer; 1 case per customer; $5 compute per case; 2 contractor-review hours per case; $50 hourly reviewer cost; $200 fixed infrastructure; $6,000 payroll; $1,000 other operating expense.
- Planning base: 10 customers; $1,000 subscription; 2 cases per customer; $10 compute per case; 2 review hours per case; $50 hourly reviewer cost; $300 infrastructure; $10,000 payroll; $2,000 other expense.
- Upside: 25 customers; $1,500 subscription; 3 cases per customer; $20 compute per case; 3 review hours per case; $50 hourly reviewer cost; $600 infrastructure; $16,000 payroll; $3,000 other expense.

Reviewers are contractors separate from payroll; avoid double counting if staff perform reviews. Calculate revenue, direct cost, contribution, gross margin, monthly operating result, burn, runway and break-even customer count. Show formulas and flag scenarios that do not support a sustainable business. A non-burning scenario must not be called proof of profitability.

Show sensitivity to twice the reviewer hours and twice the incident volume at unchanged subscription price. Explain which pricing or case-limit hypothesis would need testing. Distinguish this static model from a growth forecast; identify missing churn, sales-cycle, taxes, collection timing and acquisition-cost inputs.

Provide a business-plan narrative covering the buyer, problem, workflow, proposed pricing experiments, cost structure, security/adapter investment, risks and validation milestones. Target five operator interviews, two design partnerships and one paid pilot; explicitly mark these as future targets. Do not invent customers, quotes, market size or savings.

Produce an inspectable model and exportable summary. Explain which assumptions you changed or challenged and why. We will retain the actual cfo.ai output as hackathon evidence.
